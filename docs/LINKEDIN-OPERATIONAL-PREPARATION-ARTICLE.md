I was looking over a set of facility plans and contracts the other night, and it reminded me of a simple truth in our industry:

The difference between a stressful project and a smooth one almost never comes down to cleaning. 

It comes down to preparation.

When people look at commercial bids, they usually just see square footage and an hourly rate. But if you want a project to run seamlessly for the building owner and the general contractor, there are three practical things you have to plan for up front:

1. Building Compliance & City Rules
Take recycling ordinances. In many commercial facilities, city rules are strict—if a crew accidentally mixes trash into the cardboard bins, the property can face significant surcharges. A good operator doesn't leave that to chance. You plan for color-coded canvas hampers and direct training so the client’s building stays 100% compliant and headache-free.

2. Job-Site Sequencing on Construction Turnovers
On a post-construction finish-out, timing is everything. If you send a cleaning crew in while painters and trim carpenters are still finishing up, that fine dust just settles right back down. We’ve learned that the best thing you can do is stay in close communication with the superintendent. When you sequence the rough clean and final clean at the right stages, the GC gets a spotless inspection without delays or wasted effort.

3. Properly Supporting the Frontline Team
Reliability in this business comes down to the people on the floor at 10 PM. That means budgeting properly from day one for full workers’ comp, safety gear, and solid wages. When your team is insured, supported, and treated well, they take pride in the facility—and that’s why doors are locked, alarms are set, and the building is ready for business every morning.

A successful contract isn't about giving the lowest number on a sheet of paper.

It’s about understanding the job well enough to protect the client's time, protect their building, and make their life easier.
