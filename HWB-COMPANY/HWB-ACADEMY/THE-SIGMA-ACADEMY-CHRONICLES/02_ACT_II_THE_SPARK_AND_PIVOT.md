# Act II: The Spark & The Strategic Pivot
## *The Reusable IP Epiphany & Safe Work Habits*

---

### 2.1 The CEO's Spark
On the afternoon of September 19, 2026, while reviewing the Collin College safety requirements, CEO Humberto Dominguez issued a direct instruction:
> *"Can we create this training course? Safe Work Habits & Ergonomic Injury Prevention."*

In most companies, this request would result in an HR assistant opening Microsoft PowerPoint, pasting clip art of a person lifting a box, printing a 10-question multiple-choice quiz on paper, and filing the result in a folder.

Systems Architect George immediately recognized the broader inflection point. In Lean Six Sigma and business history, building a one-off asset is a Cardinal Waste of Talent and Resources (Muda). Why build a single slide deck for one college campus when every single commercial cleaning company, school district, hospital, and day care center in North America suffers from the exact same training deficiency?

---

### 2.2 The Strategic Discussion
Moments later, CEO Humberto Dominguez initiated the pivot:
> *"Let's go into plan mode and discuss the best way to store training courses and make them available to different projects or future clients. This may just be a great opportunity for revenue."*

This insight was the catalyst for **SigmaAcademy™**.

The fundamental realization was clear:
1. **The Commodity Trap:** Cleaning floors is a physical service. It requires direct labor, insurance, chemical consumables, and physical presence. Operating margins are capped between 15% and 25%.
2. **The Intellectual Property Highway:** Digital compliance, regulatory certification, and automated training have **zero marginal cost of reproduction**. Delivering course #1 costs the same as delivering course #10,000. Operating margins are between **85% and 92%**.
3. **The Industry Need:** Small janitorial businesses and subcontractors do not have instructional designers, software engineers, or legal compliance teams. They cannot build custom LMS platforms. If HWB built an authoritative, certified system, it could sell this compliance infrastructure to the entire market.

---

### 2.3 The Architectural Principles
To execute CEO Humberto Dominguez's vision, three foundational engineering mandates were established:
- **Centralized Database Storage:** Training courses could not be static HTML files or Word documents. They had to live in relational database tables (`AcademyCourses`, `AcademyCourseModules`, `AcademyQuizQuestions`) so they could be updated instantly and queried by autonomous agents.
- **Strict Role-Based Packaging:** Technicians should not search through a catalog of 50 courses. The system must bundle courses into targeted job roles:
  - Commercial Custodial Baseline (`PKG-CORE-W2`).
  - Campus Facility Clearance (`PKG-COLLIN-CAMPUS`).
  - Subcontractor Rapid Pass (`PKG-1099-FASTPASS`).
  - Child Care Sanitation (`PKG-DAYCARE-SAFE`).
- **Complete Backoffice Integration:** Training could not live in an isolated third-party silo (like Teachable or generic corporate HR software). It had to link directly to the Backoffice Workforce module so that assigning a training package was a one-click action directly on the candidate's W-2 or 1099 roster row.
