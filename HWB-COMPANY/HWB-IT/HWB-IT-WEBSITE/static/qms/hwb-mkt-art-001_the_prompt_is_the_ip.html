Why I Stopped Caring About the Results and Started Protecting the Instructions

Article Body

I see a lot of you using AI to write articles more often. That's great. But think about this the next time you prompt AI:

Since my team and I started incorporating these tools to get work done, it brought up a big question for me: How do we officially manage and protect all this new work we are creating with AI?

Most people think the most important part is the result—the email, the blog post, the report, the images, or even the Python script created to create something else! But I’m not talking about the "cookies" the AI bakes. I’m talking about the recipe itself—the prompt.

In my company, I’ve realized that the "prompt" (the instructions we give the AI) is now the most valuable thing we own. It’s our intellectual property.

Let me explain:

Imagine you have a robot that can clean the best floors in the world. But to get them cleaned properly without ruining them, you have to tell that robot exactly how to do it. You have to tell it which chemical to use, how hot the water should be, and even what "mood" the robot should be in while it works.

Think about a massive distribution center using crown lifts costing $75k each. Those machines have specialized wheels that can literally break if loose pebbles are left on the floor. If the robot doesn't know that specific detail, the floor isn't just "dirty"—your whole operation is at risk of a shutdown with every broken wheel.

Those precise, high-stakes instructions are the "prompt."

Our staff works very hard to "prompt" to solve everyday problems using AI. We invest in the computers, the clouds, the tools, PaaS, and the time to ensure this work delivers a real return on investment (ROI).

But here is the key: an AI-created image, a computer program, or a strategic plan created on company time with company resources is a formal asset of the company. It’s not just a "good idea"—it’s intellectual property that belongs on our balance sheet.

Keeping the Knowledge in the Safe

One of the hardest parts of running a business is when a great team member decides to move on to new opportunities. In most companies, when a person leaves, their "secret shortcuts" and best ways of working leave with them.

We decided to standardize. We have a clear set of rules (an SOP) for how to part ways while keeping our intellectual ideas safely in the vault.

When a technician or manager leaves our team, they take their skills and their experience with them, and we wish them the best. But we keep the instructions—our AI prompts. Especially the ones that protect those $75k machines. By formalizing our prompts, we ensure that the "magic" stays within the company, protecting our future and our value.

Why our Legal Team had to get involved:

I realized pretty quickly that cleaning a floor with a robot isn't just about the labor—it's about who owns the logic and the prompt. That’s why our Legal Department has a huge say in how we use AI.

Think of the Legal team as the guards of our prompt vault. They make sure that: 1. We own the logic: They ensure the work the AI does for us actually belongs to the company, not the AI provider or anyone else. 2. No one steals our patterns or prompts: They set the rules so our proprietary prompts (the ones that keep warehouses running) don't accidentally leak out. 3. Operational Safety: They make sure we aren't breaking any rules when we use the AI, keeping us safe from liability.

Tying it all together with ISO:

I decided to follow the ISO 9001 standards. To manage this effectively, I created a series of official rules (SOPs) for every department. These ensure that our AI usage isn't just a "hobby," but a formal business process.

Here is how we govern our AI DNA: * HWB-HR-002 (AI Executive Prompt Registry): Defines the roles for every AI agent. * HWB-LEG-002 (Intellectual Property Prompt Registry): Our vault for version-controlled prompts. * HWB-QMS-5.6 (AI Department Charter): The official rules for AI operations. * HWB-IT-010 (YouTube Automation Protocol): How our AI manages our digital assets.

Why this matters to me:

As a CEO, I want to build a Warchest of knowledge that stays with the company forever. I want my team to use the best tools, but I also want to make sure we own the "Digital DNA" that makes us special.

Are you protecting your high-stakes instructions, or is your AI operation a liability?
