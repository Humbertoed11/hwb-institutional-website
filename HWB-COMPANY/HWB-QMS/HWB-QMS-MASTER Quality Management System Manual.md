# Quality Management System Manual HWB Cleaning Services LLC

### 1.0 Introduction, Scope, and Objectives

#### 1.1 Company Overview
HWB Cleaning Services LLC provides reliable and affordable janitorial services for offices and commercial properties, including specialized carpet and rug cleaning. Operating in Plano and the greater DFW metroplex, our company is built on a foundation of core beliefs that guide our operations and client relationships.

**Our Vision:** To be the most trusted and respected cleaning service provider in the DFW metroplex, recognized for our exceptional quality, reliability, and commitment to our clients and employees.

**Our Mission:** Our mission is to deliver superior and reliable cleaning services to our commercial, residential, and industrial clients. We achieve this by fostering a culture of respect and safety for our team, operating with unwavering fidelity, and leveraging efficient processes to ensure complete customer satisfaction.

Our company is built on a foundation of core beliefs that guide our operations and client relationships:
*   **Respect:** For our employees and clients alike.
*   **Safety:** Ensuring all personnel are equipped with the proper training and tools to perform their duties safely.
*   **Fidelity & Responsibility:** Upholding loyalty and truthfulness in all client engagements.

Our commitment is to excellence, client satisfaction, and the continual improvement of our services.

#### 1.2 Scope of the QMS
HWB Cleaning Services LLC has established, implemented, and maintains this Quality Management System in accordance with the requirements of the ISO 9001:2015 standard. This Quality Management System (QMS) applies to all processes and services provided by HWB Cleaning Services LLC, including:
*   Provision of janitorial services for commercial properties.
*   Specialized carpet and rug cleaning services.
*   Client communication, service scheduling, and billing.
*   Procurement of cleaning supplies and equipment.
*   Training and competency management of all employees.

This QMS is designed to ensure consistent service delivery, meet customer and applicable statutory/regulatory requirements, and enhance customer satisfaction.

#### 1.3 Quality Objectives
HWB Cleaning Services LLC is committed to establishing and maintaining quality objectives that are **SMART**:
*   **S** – Specific: Clearly defined, leaving no room for ambiguity.
*   **M** – Measurable: Quantifiable, allowing for progress tracking and objective evaluation.
*   **A** – Achievable: Realistic and attainable within the organization's capabilities.
*   **R** – Relevant: Aligned with the Quality Policy and the strategic direction of HWB Cleaning Services LLC.
*   **T** – Time-based: Set with a defined timeframe for achievement.

Examples of our quality objectives include:
*   To achieve a client satisfaction rate of 95% or higher, as measured by annual surveys.
*   To ensure 100% of staff complete mandatory safety and service quality training within their first month of employment.
*   To maintain a record of zero major safety incidents throughout the fiscal year.
*   To respond to all client inquiries and service requests within 24 hours.

Our quality objectives are:
*   Documented and made available (e.g., on a 'List of Objectives').
*   Communicated and understood within HWB Cleaning Services LLC.
*   Reviewed at each management review for continuing suitability.
*   Updated as required to reflect changes in context, risks, and opportunities.

#### 4.4 Quality Management System and its Processes
In accordance with the requirements of ISO 9001:2015, HWB Cleaning Services has determined the processes needed for the QMS and their interactions. These processes are structured around the core functions of the business:

*   **Executive:** The strategic leadership and decision-making function of the company. It defines the company’s vision, sets long-term goals (including the Quality Policy and Objectives), allocates resources, and ensures the company remains profitable and compliant. This function includes the **Leadership and Management Review** process.

*   **Operations:** The execution engine of the business, where inputs are converted into services. This function manages the day-to-day delivery of cleaning services and includes the following key processes:
    *   **Service Planning and Scheduling:** Ensuring resources are available and allocated to meet client requirements.
    *   **Service Execution:** Performing the cleaning tasks to the required quality standards.
    *   **Employee Training and Competence Management:** Ensuring all staff are capable and effective.

*   **Procurement:** The sourcing and acquisition of goods and services needed for operations. This function involves identifying needs, managing vendor relationships, and purchasing supplies and equipment at the best value. This is managed through the **Procurement and Supplier Management** process.

*   **Accounting:** The financial language of the business. This function involves recording financial transactions, preparing statements, managing accounts payable/receivable, and ensuring tax compliance. While critical to the business, its processes are managed outside the direct scope of this QMS, except where they overlap with QMS processes like invoicing.

*   **Post-Delivery & Support:** The customer experience and value retention stage. This function ensures customer satisfaction and drives improvement. It includes the following key processes:
    *   **Sales and Client Onboarding:** Establishing the customer relationship and defining requirements.
    *   **Performance Evaluation and Improvement:** Gathering client feedback, handling complaints, and analyzing performance data to make the services better.

The interaction of these processes is managed to ensure seamless and high-quality service delivery.

##### 4.4.1 PDCA Cycle
This QMS is structured around the Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) cycle to ensure a framework for continual improvement. The sections of this manual correspond to the PDCA cycle as follows:
*   **Plan:** Establishing objectives and processes. This is covered in Sections 4.0, 5.0, 6.0, and 7.0.
*   **Do:** Implementing the planned processes. This is covered in Section 8.0.
*   **Check:** Monitoring and measuring processes and results. This is covered in Section 9.0.
*   **Act:** Taking actions to improve performance. This is covered in Section 10.0.

##### 4.4.2 The Process Approach
The process approach enables an organization to plan its processes and their interactions. This QMS treats all activities as interrelated processes, which function as a coherent system to achieve consistent and predictable results. For example, the "Service Delivery" process can be visualized as:
*   **Inputs:** The customer's scope of work, a trained cleaning crew, approved chemical supplies, and functional equipment.
*   **Activities:** The physical act of cleaning the client's premises according to the work order and quality standards. This includes tasks like dusting, sanitizing, vacuuming, and waste removal.
*   **Outputs:** A clean and safe environment for the client, a completed service checklist, and a satisfied customer.

This "Service Delivery" process directly interacts with other processes, such as the "Training" process (which provides the competent staff) and the "Procurement" process (which provides the approved supplies). Managing these interactions is key to quality service.

##### 4.4.3 Support Processes
The following processes support the core functions of the QMS:
*   **Resource Management:** Ensuring the availability of competent staff (Human Resources), suitable infrastructure, and an appropriate work environment.
*   **Knowledge Management:** Maintaining and sharing the organizational knowledge necessary for the operation of its processes.
*   **Control of Resources and Information:** Managing monitoring and measuring resources, communication, and all forms of documented information.
*   **Management of External Provisions:** Controlling externally provided products and services, as well as property owned by customers or external providers.
*   **Preservation and Post-Delivery:** Ensuring the preservation of outputs and managing post-delivery activities.
*   **Monitoring and Improvement:** Managing non-conformities, monitoring customer satisfaction, analyzing data, conducting internal audits, performing management reviews, and driving continual improvement.

#### 4.5 Risk-based thinking
The concept of risk-based thinking has been implicit in previous editions of this International Standard, e.g. through requirements for planning, review and improvement. This International Standard specifies requirements for the organization to understand its context (see 4.1) and determine risks as a basis for planning (see 6.1). This represents the application of risk-based thinking to planning and implementing quality management system processes (see 4.4) and will assist in determining the extent of documented information.
One of the key purposes of a quality management system is to act as a preventive tool. Consequently, this International Standard does not have a separate clause or subclause on preventive action. The concept of preventive action is expressed through the use of risk-based thinking in formulating quality management system requirements.
The risk-based thinking applied in this International Standard has enabled some reduction in prescriptive requirements and their replacement by performance-based requirements. There is greater flexibility than in ISO  9001:2008 in the requirements for processes, documented information and organizational responsibilities.
Although 6.1 specifies that the organization shall plan actions to address risks, there is no requirement for formal methods for risk management or a documented risk management process. Organizations can decide whether or not to develop a more extensive risk management methodology than is required by this International Standard, e.g. through the application of other guidance or standards.
Not all the processes of a quality management system represent the same level of risk in terms of the organization’s ability to meet its objectives, and the effects of uncertainty are not the same for all organizations. Under the requirements of 6.1, the organization is responsible for its application of risk-based thinking and the actions it takes to address risk, including whether or not to retain documented information as evidence of its determination of risks.

1.  **Identify Need:** Identify the requirement for a new document.
2.  **Delegate:** Assign creation to a competent author or person.
3.  **Draft:** Write the new document content.
4.  **Format:** Ensure the document adheres to the established format:
    *   **Header:** Company logo and Name of Document.
    *   **Footer:** Version number and Page number.
5.  **Version Control:** Update the version number in the footer (e.g., Version: 01, using a two-digit format).
6.  **Coordination:** Update any related documents affected by the new creation.
7.  **Review and Approve:** Review and approve the document for suitability and adequacy.
8.  **Master Record Update:** Update the ‘List of Documents’ with the new version number and effective date.
9.  **Distribution:** Distribute the new document to the respective personnel.

**Updating Procedure:**
1.  **Identify Need:** Identify the requirement to update an existing document.
2.  **Delegate:** Assign the update to a competent author or person.
3.  **Modify:** Modify the document content as required.
4.  **Format Check:** Verify that the header and footer format remains correct.
5.  **Version Increment:** Update the version number in the footer (e.g., Version: 02).
6.  **Coordination:** Modify any related documents as necessary.
7.  **Review and Approve:** Review and approve the updated document.
8.  **Master Record Update:** Update the ‘List of Documents’ with the new version number and effective date.
9.  **Distribution:** Distribute the updated document and ensure obsolete versions are removed from points of use.

##### 7.5.3 Control of Documented Information
Documented information required by the QMS is controlled to ensure it is available, suitable for use, and adequately protected.

**Control Aspects:**
*   **Identification:** Ensuring documents have clear titles and identifiers.
*   **Storage:** Secure physical and digital locations.
*   **Protection:** Safeguarding from loss of confidentiality, improper use, or loss of integrity.
*   **Retrieval:** Ensuring information is readily identifiable and retrievable.
*   **Retention:** Defining the period for which records are kept.
*   **Disposition:** Determining how records are archived or destroyed.

**Storage and Access:**
*   **Hard Copies:** Stored in files where each file is clearly labeled to indicate content. Files are kept in the administration offices.
*   **Soft Copies:** Stored on the network computer and/or secured cloud storage.
*   **Access Control:** Access to digital records is controlled by unique login credentials (username and password).
*   **Data Privacy:** Control of records containing sensitive client information (e.g., contact and billing data) is managed to prevent unauthorized access, in alignment with Federal Trade Commission (FTC) guidelines.

### 8.0 Operation

#### 8.1 Operational Planning and Control
Processes are in place to plan, implement, and control the operations required to meet the requirements for the provision of our cleaning services. This includes service specifications, work instructions, and quality control checkpoints.

#### 8.2 Requirements for Products and Services
HWB Cleaning Services has a process for determining, reviewing, and communicating customer requirements for services.

#### 8.3 Design and Development
This clause is not applicable to the QMS. Refer to Section 4.3.1 for justification.

#### 8.4 Control of Externally Provided Processes, Products, and Services
A process is in place for evaluating and selecting suppliers based on their ability to provide products and services in accordance with our requirements. This applies to cleaning chemicals, equipment, and any subcontracted services.

#### 8.5 Service Provision
Service provision is carried out under controlled conditions, including:
*   The availability of documented information (e.g., work orders).
*   The use of suitable equipment.
*   The implementation of monitoring and measurement activities.
*   The implementation of release, delivery, and post-delivery activities.

#### 8.6 Release of Services
Services are verified against customer requirements before completion. Records demonstrate conformity and traceability to the person(s) authorizing release.

#### 8.7 Control of Nonconforming Outputs
Services that do not conform to requirements are identified and controlled to prevent unintended use or delivery. A nonconformance report is initiated.
