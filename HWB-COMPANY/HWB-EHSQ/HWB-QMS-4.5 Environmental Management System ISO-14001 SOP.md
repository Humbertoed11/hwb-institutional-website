# 1.0 Purpose
This SOP establishes the framework for HWB Cleaning Services LLC’s Environmental Management System (EMS) in strict accordance with **ISO 14001:2015**. It ensures sustainable operations, minimizes ecological impact, and mandates the responsible lifecycle management of all cleaning agents and waste.

# 2.0 Scope
Applies to all institutional operations, chemical procurement, and waste disposal protocols executed by HWB personnel across all client facilities.

# 3.0 Procedure: Environmental Stewardship

## 3.1 Chemical Procurement and Usage
1.  **Green Certification:** Preference must be given to cleaning chemicals bearing recognized eco-certifications (e.g., Green Seal, EPA Safer Choice).
2.  **Dilution Control:** Automated dilution systems must be utilized to prevent chemical over-concentration, reducing toxic runoff and product waste.
3.  **Banned Substances:** The procurement of chemicals containing high-VOCs (Volatile Organic Compounds), phosphates, or heavy metals is strictly prohibited unless specifically required for industrial degreasing and approved by the EHSQ Department.

## 3.2 Waste Management and Disposal
1.  **Recycling Protocols:** All cardboard, plastics, and recyclable materials generated during post-construction or daily operations must be sorted and disposed of in designated recycling streams.
2.  **Hazardous Waste:** Any biologically contaminated or chemically hazardous waste must be sealed and disposed of in accordance with local municipal and federal EPA regulations.

## 3.3 Carbon Footprint Reduction
1.  **Route Optimization:** The Logistics and Operations departments must utilize algorithmic route planning for mobile cleaning units to minimize fuel consumption.

# 4.0 Verification
*   **Annual Audit:** The EHSQ Department will conduct an annual environmental impact audit to verify compliance with ISO 14001.
*   **SDS Tracking:** Safety Data Sheets (SDS) for all active chemicals must be maintained and verified quarterly.

# 5.0 Notes and Cautions
*   **Zero-Muda:** Environmental waste is considered a form of "Muda" (waste) within the Lean Six Sigma framework and must be continuously reduced.
